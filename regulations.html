Support Fund Regulations

Hope Association provides interest-free loans and contributions to people in need, so they can build a livelihood and stand on their own feet. No interest is charged and we make no profit — every amount paid back goes straight into the fund to help the next person. These regulations set out who can be supported, what for, who decides, and how repayment works.

🎯

1. Purpose

Verein Hope Bern operates a support fund for people in need, mainly in India, Nepal, Mexico, Pakistan and Syria. The fund enables people to build their own livelihood and to live in dignity.

The fund provides two kinds of assistance:

  • Interest-free loans to individuals and families, to create or secure a livelihood.
  • Work contributions to people who carry out social work voluntarily and on their own responsibility. A work contribution is a grant without consideration in return and does not create an employment or service relationship.
🤝

2. Principles

Interest-free: no interest, fees or commission of any kind is charged. Only the amount received is repaid.

  • No commercial purpose — the association makes no profit from the fund.
  • Revolving character — repayments remain in the fund and are used to support others.
  • No benefit to members — members and governing bodies of the association receive no assistance from the fund.
🫱

3. Who is supported

Assistance is given to people who are in need and who have no access to fair and affordable financing. Need is the sole criterion.

Assistance is given regardless of religion, caste, ethnic background, gender and political conviction. Membership of a community, organisation or religion is neither required nor expected. No service in return is requested.

Recipients may also be local partners who carry out coordinating tasks for the association. Section 6 applies to such cases.

🌱

4. Permitted use

Assistance is given in particular for:

  • Setting up a small trade or self-employed activity
  • Means of earning a living, such as vehicles or equipment used to generate income
  • Land used for subsistence or to generate income
  • Education, school and college fees, vocational training
  • Medical treatment that would otherwise be out of reach
  • Housing: rental deposits as well as building and repair work on the family's own home
  • Release from debts owed to money lenders charging ruinous interest
  • Unavoidable family obligations, in particular the marriage of a child, where without assistance the family would otherwise borrow from money lenders at ruinous interest. What matters is not the occasion but preventing the family from falling into debt.
  • Social initiatives for the benefit of a community

Excluded are:

  • Investment, speculation and the acquisition of assets for investment purposes
  • Businesses aimed at maximising profit rather than securing a family's livelihood
  • Consumer spending without lasting benefit
  • Political and religious worship purposes
📋

5. Application and decision

Applications are submitted in writing using the designated form. They state the applicant's circumstances, the need, the intended use and the proposed repayment.

The local coordinator reviews the application and gives an opinion. Decisions are taken according to the following levels of authority:

AmountDecision-making authority
up to CHF 5,000Managing Director alone
CHF 5,000 to 15,000Managing Director and one board member
above CHF 15,000The Board
Applications under section 6The Board, without the person concerned

The thresholds are set by the Board and may be adjusted.

Co-funding partner organisations: part of the funds comes from partner organisations, in particular Verein Seeds and Alive. Where an application is funded wholly or partly by a partner organisation, the decision is taken in consultation with that organisation. The consultation and the persons involved are documented. The source of funds is recorded for every payment.

Every decision is recorded with date, amount, purpose and the persons deciding. Rejections are documented as well.

⚖️

6. Conflicts of interest and recusal

People who carry out coordinating or operational tasks for the association may receive assistance from the fund. The same conditions apply to them as to all other recipients; more generous amounts or easier repayment terms are not permitted.

Applications from such people, and applications from people close to them, are decided by the Board. The person concerned, and the Managing Director where he or she has a business or personal connection with that person, must recuse themselves.

Existing business relationships between officers of the association and recipients are disclosed to the Board and recorded in the minutes.

Overlapping roles with partner organisations: where a person holds positions both in Verein Hope Bern and in a co-funding partner organisation, this is disclosed to both bodies and noted in the minutes. In joint decisions, that person's vote counts only once.

💙

7. Repayment

  • Repayment is made in agreed monthly amounts, set according to the recipient's economic situation.
  • No collateral is required.
  • No default interest, reminder fees or penalties are charged. The association uses no debt collection agencies.
  • In case of illness, crop failure or other hardship, repayment may be suspended or adjusted.
  • In cases of serious hardship the remaining amount may be waived in whole or in part. A waiver is documented with reasons and decided according to the levels of authority in section 5.
📒

8. Loan and work contribution

Loans and work contributions are legally and financially separate transactions. A loan is a receivable of the association; a work contribution is a grant without consideration in return.

Where the two are set off against each other and only the difference is transferred, each must still be recorded separately: the work contribution as an expense, the repayment as an inflow against the receivable. Net settlement without separate recording is not permitted.

🏛️

9. Records and accountability

  • Applications, decisions, payments and repayments are recorded in full.
  • Recipients of work contributions submit a short annual report on their activity.
  • Payments are made directly to the recipient or organisation wherever possible. Where this is not possible for legal or practical reasons, the payment route is justified in writing and the onward transfer is evidenced.
  • The balance of the fund, the assistance granted and the repayments received are disclosed in the annual accounts.
